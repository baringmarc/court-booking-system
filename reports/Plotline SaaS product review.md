# Make Plotline easier to trust and run

*Review date: 12 November 2026, Asia/Manila. Scope: court-booking-system and plotline-site as one product.*

**Plotline has a credible core for a closely supported, manual-payment pilot, but it has not yet demonstrated readiness for broader commercial rollout.** The booking engine and owner tools are considerably more developed than the sales proof and operating processes around them. The main improvements should make money, booking status, staff responsibilities and recovery unmistakably clear. The visual identity is already coherent; a wholesale redesign would deliver less value than fixing mobile hierarchy and daily workflows. The most urgent verified mismatch is that marketing describes venue-owned automatic PayMongo payments while the reviewed implementation enforces a shared test-mode account. My recommendation is to spend the next stage proving that a few real venues can run their week reliably, then use that evidence to choose expansion features. This is a product assessment, not a certification of security, production configuration or profitability.

## Preserve the strong core and repair six weak links

Guest booking without compulsory registration, server-authoritative prices, overlap protection, stale-selection recovery and practical GCash/Maya/bank payment helpers are valuable foundations. The owner product already includes a setup checklist, publish readiness checks, payment review, staff rescheduling, configurable walk-ins, occupancy and earnings views, CSV exports, statements and invoicing. Recommending “add reporting” or “add an onboarding checklist” would miss what is already built. ([Checkout](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/CheckoutPage.tsx:69), [Setup checklist](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/admin/SetupChecklist.tsx:7), [Earnings](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/admin/EarningsSection.tsx:32), [Statements](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/admin/StatementPage.tsx:37))

There is also careful engineering worth preserving: venue-scoped access rules, cross-venue test coverage, protected guest links, CSV formula-injection defenses, accessibility tests, error monitoring and off-site backup code. These are evidence of deliberate implementation, although this audit did not rerun tests or prove the current deployment. Rewriting the stack or splitting it into microservices would not address the clearest problems found. ([Tenant rules](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/supabase/migrations/20261030062110_venue_scoped_rls.sql:1), [Guest links](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/guestLink.ts:1), [CSV safeguards](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/admin/csv.ts:11), [Accessibility tests](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/e2e/a11y.spec.ts:1))

The following priorities connect the two repositories rather than treating marketing, booking and operations as separate projects. Effort is relative scope, not a delivery estimate.

| Priority | Improvement | Why it matters | Effort and dependency | Evidence of success |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Align payment promises with available capability | Prevents owners buying an expectation the current product cannot fulfill | Small copy/release review; live payments are separate, large work | Every public claim maps to demonstrated capability or an explicit future label |
| 2 | Clarify total price, reservation status and payment review | Addresses uncertainty at the point of commitment | Small–medium UI work; policy settings need backend work | Players can explain amount, status and next step; fewer incomplete payments |
| 3 | Put available slots and urgent owner actions first | Reduces scrolling and navigation friction | Small–medium layout/navigation work | Faster valid slot selection and booking lookup on phones |
| 4 | Track refunds and give staff appropriate permissions | Makes exceptions accountable and limits unnecessary access | Medium–large data/security work | Exceptions close with evidence; desk staff cannot change payout settings |
| 5 | Finish coordinated releases, recovery and exit procedures | Makes reliability repeatable across venues | Medium operational work; build on M16 | Dependency checks stop invalid releases; recovery and offboarding rehearsals pass |
| 6 | Prove activation, repeat use and venue economics | Establishes what to build and whether the model sustains service | Small measurement setup, ongoing pilot work | First real booking, week-four use, support workload and collected contribution are visible |

## Make every player step answer what happens next

**The first price should be the complete payable price.** The selection bar sums court time; checkout adds the booking fee. At the documented ₱10 fee, an ₱800 selection becomes ₱810 at checkout. This is a presentation mismatch rather than incorrect arithmetic. Keep hourly court prices in the grid, but show “Court ₱800 + booking fee ₱10 = total ₱810” once a selection exists, using the same fee source as checkout. Reconcile normal prices, promotions and fee changes throughout the journey. ([Selection calculation](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/selection.ts:75), [Selection display](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/SelectionBar.tsx:27), [Checkout fee](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/CheckoutPage.tsx:123))

The next problem is vocabulary. **“Confirm booking” currently creates an unpaid reservation**, while the following page correctly says “Booking reserved.” Change the action to “Reserve and continue to payment,” then show a compact progression: reserved → payment submitted → confirmed. On an unpaid reservation, the total, deadline and payment action should lead. The large save-link panel currently precedes the summary and payment; retain the recovery function in a compact notice. On confirmation, lead with date, time, location and arrival instructions. ([Checkout action](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/CheckoutForm.tsx:84), [Booking hierarchy](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/BookingPage.tsx:54))

Manual payment deserves one guided path. Today account cards and the proof form’s “Paid with” selection are separate. Let players choose a method once, reveal that account’s QR/details, and carry it into proof submission. Preserve copying, QR downloads, image previews and the iPhone saving hint: these are useful local details. After upload, replace the generic checking message with a receipt time, realistic review expectation, notification channel and venue contact action. The venue needs an overdue-proof queue behind that promise; do not display a review-time guarantee that staff cannot meet. ([Payment accounts](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/PaymentAccountList.tsx:20), [Proof form](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/ProofForm.tsx:65), [Pending status](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/BookingSummary.tsx:101))

**Cancellation terms should appear before reservation.** The current checkout link depends on finding a freeform venue section whose title matches “cancel”; the exact deadline appears later, and a paid cancellation directs the player to the venue about refunds. Introduce structured cutoff and refund-treatment settings, a short checkout disclosure, and a stored policy snapshot. This recommendation concerns clarity and supportability; it is not a legal-compliance finding. Pair it with manual refund tracking rather than waiting for automated refunds. ([Policy link logic](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/venue/VenueInfo.tsx:144), [Cancellation wording](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/CancelBooking.tsx:19))

Recovery and return visits need attention too. Expired reservations say users can book again without a direct rebooking action; guest-link email requests discard errors in the client hook. Provide “Try this time again” with fresh availability, alternatives when unavailable, and a privacy-preserving link recovery process. Then add calendar export, a pre-session reminder and “Book this court again.” Source searches found lifecycle emails but no scheduled pre-session reminder or calendar export. These are proposals, not existing capabilities, and must handle cancellation/rescheduling without duplicate or stale messages. ([Expired state](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/BookingSummary.tsx:113), [Guest email request](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/hooks.ts:44), [My bookings](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/account/MyBookingsPage.tsx:55), [Email kinds](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/supabase/functions/_shared/emailTemplates.ts:5))

## Keep the visual identity and change the hierarchy

The live marketing and demo pages share a credible visual language: warm colors, Fraunces headings, readable text and recognizable controls. **The clearest observed design weakness is how long it takes to reach the task on mobile.** At 390×844, the live booking page initially showed the venue header, date card, sport controls and expanded court photos, with no availability grid visible. Even after collapsing the gallery, the grid header only began near the screen’s bottom. This was a live observation, not a claim inferred only from CSS. ([Live demo](https://claudex-courts.plotline.fit/book))

Compress the date controls, keep court details collapsed on booking, and reserve prominent photography for the venue’s home page. Aim to expose useful slots sooner while retaining a readable selection summary. Test a narrow-screen available-times list against the grid with real players before replacing the grid entirely. Keep accessible labels, visible focus, non-color status cues and adequate touch targets; the existing implementation already provides useful foundations. Filters such as morning/evening or “next available” should earn their place through task testing. ([Booking page order](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/BookPage.tsx:119), [Grid semantics](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/SlotGrid.tsx:11))

The owner dashboard needs similar discipline. Earnings precede today’s grid and schedule, and the summary links all point to the bare bookings route, whose default is the proof queue. Clicking today’s count or the next booking should take the owner to that specific context. Make the default workspace answer: what needs attention, who arrives next, and what is free today? Move broad earnings analysis into a clearly named reports area; currently “Reports” routes to the Plotline statement while business analytics remain on Dashboard. ([Dashboard](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/admin/DashboardPage.tsx:103), [Default filter](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/admin/bookingFilters.ts:26), [Routes](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/routes/router.tsx:80))

On the sales site, show an owner outcome sooner. The live page already has an admin screenshot tour and video control; these should be promoted, not rebuilt. Use distinct actions such as “Watch owner demo” and “Try player booking.” The long page repeats commercial framing across several sections, while the mobile first screen contains mostly copy and promises. Put a compact real product preview near the hero and let one pricing section carry detailed terms. Existing drawn screens can remain, but their prices and booking states should match reality. ([Live tour](https://plotline.fit/#features), [Page structure](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/plotline-site/src/routes/LandingPage.tsx:21), [Illustrated phone](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/plotline-site/src/components/PhoneMockup.tsx:38))

Treat privacy as a product choice in this cleanup. Public slots currently display bookers’ first names. Reconsider whether a neutral “Booked” label should be the default, with any community benefit explicitly explained. No legal violation is asserted here. ([Public slot labels](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/booking/SlotGrid.tsx:102))

## Make the service dependable beyond the happy path

The marketing payment mismatch is precise: site copy describes automatic PayMongo confirmation through the owner’s account; checkout reads one global secret, rejects live credentials, and the console describes a shared test account. **Test checkout infrastructure is implemented; venue-owned live automatic payments are not implemented in the reviewed source.** Qualify the sales wording now. Complete per-venue live payment onboarding only as a separate release with account checks, webhook reconciliation and controlled transaction evidence. Hosted switches and secrets were not inspected, and this review makes no claim that unauthorized charges occurred. ([Marketing claims](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/plotline-site/src/config/site.ts:463), [Checkout key enforcement](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/supabase/functions/create-paymongo-checkout/index.ts:29), [Test-key helper](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/supabase/functions/_shared/paymongo.ts:20), [Console warning](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/console/OnlinePaymentsControl.tsx:40))

Refunds need an accountable completion state. The app warns about paid cancellations and flags certain online payments as refund due, but resolution happens outside the product. Add amount, reason, responsible person, due date, external reference and settled timestamp. Keep original payment records intact and distinguish gross collected, refunded and net receipts. This benefits manual GCash/bank refunds immediately; it does not depend on live PayMongo. ([Review warnings](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/admin/ReviewPanel.tsx:65), [Refund flags](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/admin/paymentFlags.ts:18))

Owner and staff roles also need practical separation before broad multi-staff adoption. Membership distinguishes the labels, but the main admin helper accepts membership generally and many settings policies use it. Introduce a small permission matrix covering desk bookings, payment review, prices, payment accounts, customer exports and settings; add invitations and revocation. Enforce permissions in the database and test owner, staff and cross-venue behavior. This is a least-privilege improvement, not evidence of a cross-tenant breach. ([Admin helper](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/supabase/migrations/20261030054219_venues_members_platform_admins.sql:45), [Settings policies](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/supabase/migrations/20261030062110_venue_scoped_rls.sql:1))

Release coordination deserves priority because the Handoff records two repaired November incidents involving frontends arriving before required database/functions. A release manifest should tie app version, migrations, functions and marketing contract together; deployment preflight should reject missing dependencies. Add post-release availability and authenticated owner/console smoke checks. Finish the postponed M16 monitoring/runbook work rather than creating a duplicate initiative. These are documented historical incidents, not a claim the site remains broken. ([Release history and M16](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/TASKS.md), [Shared contract](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/docs/PLATFORM.md))

Backups exist, but recovery proof should go beyond public-table counts. Rehearse restored photos/payment proofs, access rules and a booking lifecycle in a disposable environment; record recovery time and acceptable data loss. Supabase’s database backups do not include Storage objects, making the existing separate Storage archive valuable. Verify its recovery as well as its creation. ([Backup script](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/scripts/backup-production.mjs:35), [Restore checker](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/scripts/restore-check.mjs:24), [Supabase backup guidance](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups))

Finally, fulfill exit promises before the first real exit. Public terms describe closure, final billing, 30 days of export access and subsequent deletion subject to retention. Current guarded deletion deliberately rejects venues with history; it is not a customer offboarding lifecycle. Start with a reviewed manual case process covering unresolved bookings/refunds, export access, retained records and deadlines, then automate. Preserve deletion safeguards. ([Public terms](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/plotline-site/src/config/legal.ts:80), [Deletion guard](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/supabase/migrations/20261106180458_t17_26_guarded_deletion.sql:140))

## Win through demonstrable operations, then expand selectively

Plotline’s strongest proposed position is straightforward court operations for small Philippine venues, with familiar payments and hands-on setup. **Low fees and guest checkout help, but they are not unique.** Competitor capabilities below are official vendor claims reviewed for this audit, not independently tested accounts or transactions.

| Comparator | Relevant advertised capability | Lesson for Plotline |
|---|---|---|
| [Taradula](https://taradula.com/list-your-venue) | No setup/monthly fee, direct GCash, published ₱15 player fee; walk-ins, recurrence, player history and restricted staff access | Closest commercial comparison. Prove easier operations and service rather than relying on a ₱5 difference. |
| [Sparrk](https://www.sparrk.ph/) | Philippine discovery, open play, event payments, roster/queue tools and walk-in calendar | Open play could be a meaningful expansion if pilot venues need attendance management. |
| [CourtReserve](https://courtreserve.com/) | Broad reservations, events, memberships, lessons, billing and reporting; [public guest booking](https://help.courtreserve.com/en/articles/11787240-public-booking-setup-guide-and-overview) | Guest access is competitive parity; avoid copying the entire mature club suite. |
| [Playtomic](https://helpmanager.playtomic.com/hc/en-gb/articles/20535331224721-Split-Payment-vs-Single-payment) | Split-payment rules include channel/sport limits and responsibility for unpaid shares | Split payments create collection and liability workflows, not just a checkout option. |
| [AllBooked](https://www.allbooked.com/) | Recurrence, quotas, cancellation controls, check-in prompts and analytics | Benchmark useful operating rules once demand justifies their complexity. |

Acquisition currently asks for seven required data inputs before a conversation and promises follow-up “soon.” Shorten initial contact to the minimum needed to respond, collecting setup detail progressively. This needs a shared form-contract change, not deletion of frontend fields alone. Keep assisted onboarding: add existing-booking migration, a practice payment review, approved public schedule and first real booking to the activation process. Assign lead follow-up and measure time to first contact, publication and first confirmed booking. ([Contact form](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/plotline-site/src/features/contact/ContactForm.tsx:93), [Follow-up copy](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/plotline-site/src/config/site.ts:538), [Lead operations](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/console/LeadsPage.tsx:16))

The live empty review section is honest but weak proof. Replace its empty state with a truthful pilot invitation or guided-setup assurance; collect permissioned stories from two or three venues, measuring approval speed, coordination effort and continued use. Qualify “Booked in a minute” as submission time unless confirmation speed is demonstrated. Put optional processor fees beside the pricing example, not only in a later FAQ. ([Reviews](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/plotline-site/src/features/reviews/ReviewsSection.tsx:14), [Speed claim](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/plotline-site/src/config/site.ts:119), [Pricing copy](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/plotline-site/src/config/site.ts:355))

Measure the full chain: visit → demo → lead → configured venue → first booking → week-four active venue. Separately measure selection → reservation → proof → confirmation, review latency, unresolved refunds and repeat booking. No product-funnel event integration was found in the inspected source; that does not establish absence of hosting analytics. Keep names, proof contents and guest tokens out of events, and review privacy wording before adding analytics. SEO can follow with prerendered public content, canonical URLs, sitemap and useful local venue-owner content; client rendering is not proof that Google cannot index the site. ([Privacy wording](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/plotline-site/src/config/legal.ts:139), [Public HTML](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/plotline-site/index.html:9))

The economics need measurement before a pricing decision. At the current ₱10 fee, 100, 300 and 1,000 billable transactions produce ₱1,000, ₱3,000 and ₱10,000 in gross fee charges, before credits, collection losses, support, infrastructure and tax. Track collected contribution per active venue and support minutes per 100 bookings. Free setup remains a useful offer only if setup and ongoing service costs are understood; no fee change is recommended from the available evidence. ([Business facts](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/docs/facts.json), [Billing rules](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/docs/SAAS-PLAN.md:109))

A practical sequence is **first 30 days:** correct capability claims, improve payment/mobile clarity, rehearse releases and recovery, and instrument a small pilot. **Days 31–60:** improve refund resolution, staff permissions, onboarding and exit handling; add lightweight reminders/calendar/rebooking. **Days 61–90:** choose expansion from repeated pilot needs and economics. These are proposed planning windows, not capacity-backed commitments. Existing admin rescheduling should be retained; a player change request can precede complex self-service repricing. Recurring series, open-play capacity/registration and waitlists are worthwhile candidates, but each needs explicit conflict, cancellation and billing rules. Open play currently directs people to external joining links; recurring bookings are deliberately outside current scope. ([Staff rescheduling](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/admin/RescheduleSection.tsx:18), [Open play](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/src/features/openPlay/OpenPlayDialog.tsx:53), [Non-goals](/Users/macbookpro/Documents/Projects/court-booking-system/SPEC.md:15))

Defer native apps, broad memberships/leagues/coaching, full POS, AI booking assistants, split payments and a national marketplace until repeated customer demand makes their maintenance worthwhile. The decisive proof is whether owners stop keeping a parallel notebook and players can finish a booking without clarification. Five representative players and two owners completing realistic phone tasks would provide a better basis for the next design milestone than another aesthetic refresh.

Traffic, conversion, support costs and retention were unavailable; proposed benefits and metrics are hypotheses, not measured improvements. The live admin tour/video control was present despite older Handoff wording; playback was not tested.
